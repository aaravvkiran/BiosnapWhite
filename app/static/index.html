📸 Scanning Best Practices & Tips
01
Close-up Framing: Fill at least 40% of the camera frame with the target insect, leaf, or bark marking.
02
Check Key Markings: Focus on wing spots, white antennae bands, or leaf vein patterns for lookalike disambiguation.
03
Natural Daylight: Avoid harsh camera flash glare; steady, diffuse daylight produces highest AI confidence scores.
04
Multiple Angles: For beetles and caterpillars, capture top-down dorsal and profile views when safe.

Protecting Our Local Ecosystems

Invasive species pose one of the greatest threats to global biodiversity, second only to habitat destruction. They outcompete native plants and animals, alter soil chemistry, and can cause significant economic damage.

Our Mission

To empower communities with accessible technology to rapidly identify, report, and manage invasive species before they establish permanent footholds in native habitats.

🛡️ Volunteer Safety & Ethics Principles

  • Wear appropriate field attire: Sturdy closed-toe boots, long pants tucked into socks, and heavy-duty work gloves for pulling thorny vines.
  • Tick prevention: Apply EPA-registered insect repellent and perform tick checks after walking in tall grass or brush.
  • Do not touch unknown stinging insects: Report suspected Asian giant hornets or aggressive yellowjacket nests directly through the app without approaching.
  • Respect property boundaries: Only survey on public conservation land, state parks, or with explicit landowner permission.
Questions? Contact the Worcester County Volunteer Stewardship Desk at volunteer@ecosnap.org or call (508) 792-7711 ext. 4.
